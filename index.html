THE COLLECTOR'S NOTES

Buy with curiosity.
Collect with confidence.

The right watch is only half the story.
Get to know its history—and its seller.

01 Antique or vintage?+

In this collection, “antique” means at least 100 years old. Later historical watches are labelled vintage. Vendor dates are often approximate; a decade is shown as a range rather than an invented exact year.

02 Ask about the details.+

Request movement photographs, service records, serial numbers and a written condition report. Ask whether the dial, hands, crown, case or bracelet have been replaced or restored. A beautiful patina and a refinished dial tell different stories.

03 Make the journey clear.+

Before paying, confirm the currency, insurance, shipping destination, import duties, payment method, return window and warranty directly with the vendor. Availability shown here is a research snapshot, so always check the original listing.

04 Care for a little history.+

Old water-resistance ratings are not a present-day promise. Ask the seller about safe daily use, winding, servicing and storage. Keep delicate historic watches away from water, strong magnets and shocks.
